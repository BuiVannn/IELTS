## Cụm từ

- **consumer culture** — văn hoá tiêu dùng — Consumer culture encourages people to replace their phones every year even when the old one still works.
- **impulse buying** — mua sắm bốc đồng — Flash sales on Shopee are designed to trigger impulse buying.
- **live beyond their means** — sống vượt quá khả năng tài chính — Many young people live beyond their means to keep up with friends on social media.
- **status symbols** — biểu tượng địa vị — Luxury cars and designer handbags are seen as status symbols in big cities.
- **a throwaway culture** — văn hoá dùng xong vứt — Cheap fast fashion has created a throwaway culture where clothes are worn only a few times.
- **disposable income** — thu nhập khả dụng (sau chi phí thiết yếu) — As disposable income rises, families spend more on travel and eating out.
- **stick to a budget** — chi tiêu theo ngân sách — Students who stick to a budget rarely run out of money before the end of the month.
- **put money aside** — để dành tiền — I try to put money aside every month for emergencies.
- **save for a rainy day** — tiết kiệm phòng khi khó khăn — My parents always told me to save for a rainy day, and the pandemic proved them right.
- **run up debt** — nợ chồng chất — It is easy to run up debt on credit cards when you pay only the minimum each month.
- **buy now, pay later** — mua trước trả sau — Buy now, pay later schemes make expensive items feel cheaper than they really are.
- **imported goods** — hàng nhập khẩu — Imported goods such as Australian beef are often seen as safer and higher in quality.
- **domestic producers** — nhà sản xuất trong nước — Cheap foreign products can push domestic producers out of business.
- **food self-sufficiency** — tự chủ lương thực — Vietnam has achieved food self-sufficiency in rice and is now a major exporter.
- **income inequality** — bất bình đẳng thu nhập — Income inequality is visible when luxury flats stand next to tiny rented rooms.
- **a minimum wage** — mức lương tối thiểu — Raising a minimum wage helps factory workers but may make some firms hire fewer people.
- **e-commerce platforms** — sàn thương mại điện tử — E-commerce platforms allow small sellers in rural areas to reach buyers nationwide.
- **value for money** — đáng đồng tiền — Local markets often offer better value for money than supermarkets.
- **materialistic values** — lối sống coi trọng vật chất — Advertising can spread materialistic values among children who judge friends by their trainers.
- **stimulate economic growth** — thúc đẩy tăng trưởng kinh tế — Consumer spending helps stimulate economic growth, especially during festivals like Tet.

## Kho ý tưởng Task 2

### Quảng cáo bán thứ người ta không cần

Đề (04/04/2026; 10/01, Opinion): The main aim of advertising is to sell products that people do not really need. Agree or disagree?

**Đồng ý**
- Creates artificial needs → ads link products to happiness and status → e.g. phone adverts suggest last year's model is out of date.
- Targets impulse buying → personalised ads on social media appear exactly when people are bored or tired.

**Không đồng ý**
- Informs consumers → people learn about new products, prices and discounts → e.g. ads for health insurance or vaccines.
- Helps small businesses compete → a new café can attract customers through cheap online ads.
- Public service adverts promote safety → e.g. campaigns against drink-driving.

### Ai cũng muốn ô tô, TV, tủ lạnh

Đề (16/06/2026, Adv–Disadv): Nowadays everyone wants to own a car, a television and a fridge. Do the disadvantages outweigh the advantages?

**Lợi ích**
- Better quality of life → fridges keep food safe and reduce daily trips to the market.
- Stimulates economic growth → demand creates factory and retail jobs → e.g. car assembly plants in Vietnam.

**Bất lợi**
- Environmental cost → more cars mean more emissions and traffic jams.
- Debt and pressure → families run up debt to buy status symbols → e.g. taking out a loan for a car they rarely drive in a congested city.
- Materialistic values → people measure success by possessions.

> Gợi ý: bất lợi lớn hơn với ô tô ở thành phố đông, nhưng tủ lạnh và TV là nhu cầu chính đáng.

### Thói quen mua sắm và độ tuổi (mua sắm online)

Đề (12/09/2026, Opinion): Shopping habits are affected more by age group than by any other factor. Agree or disagree?

**Đồng ý**
- Young people prefer e-commerce platforms → they trust reviews and mobile payments → e.g. students buy almost everything on Shopee or TikTok Shop.
- Older people prefer touching products and talking to sellers → they still go to wet markets daily.

**Không đồng ý**
- Income matters more → people with high disposable income buy brands regardless of age.
- Location → rural shoppers of all ages have fewer shops, so they rely more on online delivery.
- Culture and habits → e.g. many retired people now shop online after learning during the pandemic.

### Thực phẩm nhập khẩu và tự chủ lương thực

Đề (đề quen thuộc, Opinion): Countries should produce all the food they need instead of importing it. Agree or disagree?

**Đồng ý**
- Food security → supply chains can break → e.g. during COVID-19 some countries limited food exports.
- Supports domestic producers and rural jobs.

**Không đồng ý**
- Not realistic → climate and land limit what can be grown → e.g. Vietnam cannot easily grow apples or wheat on a large scale.
- Trade is efficient → each country exports what it grows best → e.g. Vietnam exports rice and coffee and imports dairy and wheat.
- Imported goods give consumers more choice and keep prices competitive.

### Chênh lệch thu nhập: có nên giới hạn lương cao?

Đề (đề quen thuộc, Opinion): Some people earn far more than others. Governments should limit the salaries of top earners. Agree or disagree?

**Đồng ý**
- Reduces income inequality → extra tax revenue can fund schools and hospitals.
- Huge salaries are not always deserved → e.g. bosses receive bonuses even when companies lose money.

**Không đồng ý**
- Talent and risk deserve reward → founders and surgeons invest years of training.
- Brain drain → top workers move abroad or to other firms.
- Better solutions → progressive taxes and a fair minimum wage rather than a cap.

### Tiết kiệm hay chi tiêu

Đề (đề quen thuộc, Discussion): Some people think young people should save money for the future, while others believe they should spend it and enjoy life. Discuss both views.

**Nên tiết kiệm**
- Financial security → savings protect against job loss or illness → e.g. many people had no savings when they lost income in 2020.
- Long-term goals → saving early helps people buy a home or start a business.

**Nên chi tiêu**
- Experiences have lasting value → travel and courses build skills and memories.
- Investing in yourself → e.g. paying for an IELTS course can lead to a higher salary.

> Gợi ý: kết luận: put money aside trước (ví dụ 20% thu nhập), phần còn lại chi tiêu có kế hoạch.

## Speaking dự đoán

**Do you prefer shopping online or in shops? (Part 1 – Shopping)**

These days I do most of my shopping online because it **saves me a lot of time** and the prices are often lower. I can **read reviews** and compare several sellers in a few minutes. Having said that, for clothes and shoes I still prefer going to a shop, because I like to **try things on** before I **pay for** them.

**Do you compare prices before buying? (Part 1 – Shopping)**

Yes, almost always, especially for **big-ticket items** like a laptop or a phone. I usually check two or three websites and wait for **a sales event**, because the difference can be quite large. For small things like snacks, though, I don't **bother** at all.

**Why do some people buy expensive watches? (Part 1 – Watches)**

I think for many people it's more about **image** than telling the time, since everyone has a phone anyway. A luxury watch is **a way of showing** success, especially in business meetings. Some people also see it as **an investment**, because certain brands **hold their value** really well.

**Does money make people happy? (Part 3 – Person)**

Up to a point, yes. Having enough money to **cover basic needs** and not worry about bills definitely **reduces stress**. But once people are reasonably comfortable, I don't think more money makes **a huge difference**. Relationships, health and **a sense of purpose** seem to matter much more in the long run.

**Is good customer service more important than price? (Part 3 – Experience)**

It depends on what you're buying. For cheap, everyday items, most people simply **go for the lowest price**. But for expensive products like a car or a fridge, good service, such as **after-sales support** and repairs, can **make or break** the decision. Personally, I'm happy to **pay a bit extra** if I know the shop will help me when something goes wrong.

**Should children be taught about money at school? (Part 3 – Experience)**

I strongly believe they should. Many young adults **get into debt** simply because nobody taught them how interest or credit cards work. Schools could teach **basic financial skills** like budgeting through simple projects, for example running a small class shop. That would help children **develop good habits** early on.
