## Cụm từ

- **job satisfaction** — sự hài lòng với công việc — For many young people, job satisfaction now matters more than a high starting salary.
- **job security** — sự ổn định công việc — My parents chose state jobs mainly for the job security they offered.
- **a stable income** — thu nhập ổn định — A stable income allows a young couple to plan for a home and children.
- **interpersonal skills** — kỹ năng giao tiếp, ứng xử với người khác — Strong interpersonal skills helped her win clients even though her degree was in chemistry.
- **paper qualifications** — bằng cấp trên giấy — Paper qualifications may get you an interview, but they rarely get you promoted.
- **climb the career ladder** — thăng tiến trong sự nghiệp — He climbed the career ladder quickly by volunteering for difficult projects.
- **a dead-end job** — công việc không có tương lai — She left a dead-end job at a call centre to retrain as a web developer.
- **switch careers** — chuyển nghề — More people in their thirties now switch careers after taking short online courses.
- **work remotely** — làm việc từ xa — Since 2021 many software engineers in Ho Chi Minh City have been allowed to work remotely three days a week.
- **strike a healthy work-life balance** — cân bằng công việc và cuộc sống — Flexible hours helped him strike a healthy work-life balance after his son was born.
- **burn out** — kiệt sức vì làm việc quá sức — Junior auditors often burn out during the busy season from January to March.
- **paternity leave** — chế độ nghỉ phép cho người bố — In Vietnam fathers get only a few days of paternity leave after their child is born.
- **share childcare responsibilities** — chia sẻ việc chăm con — Longer leave for fathers encourages couples to share childcare responsibilities more equally.
- **be made redundant** — bị cho nghỉ việc (do cắt giảm) — Factory workers fear they will be made redundant if orders from Europe keep falling.
- **acquire new skills** — học thêm kỹ năng mới — Workers who acquire new skills regularly are less likely to lose their jobs to automation.
- **a competitive salary** — mức lương cạnh tranh — Foreign firms attract top graduates by offering a competitive salary and health insurance.
- **job prospects** — triển vọng nghề nghiệp — Job prospects for nurses remain excellent because of rising demand for healthcare.
- **pursue a career in** — theo đuổi sự nghiệp trong ngành — My cousin wants to pursue a career in graphic design, despite her parents' doubts.
- **the gig economy** — nền kinh tế việc làm tự do, ngắn hạn — Delivery riders for Grab and ShopeeFood are a typical part of the gig economy.

## Kho ý tưởng Task 2

### Kỹ năng xã hội quan trọng như bằng cấp

Đề (06/09/2026; 13/06/2026, Opinion): Social skills are as important as qualifications for success in a job. Agree or disagree?

**Ủng hộ**
- Most jobs involve people → staff must negotiate, explain and handle complaints → e.g. a bank teller with a finance degree still loses customers if she cannot stay calm with an angry client.
- Teamwork drives results → projects fail because of poor communication more often than poor knowledge → e.g. a software release delayed because two teams did not share information.
- Promotion depends on leadership → managers are chosen for how they motivate others → e.g. the best engineer is not always the best head of department.

**Phản đối**
- Some jobs need technical expertise first → no amount of charm can replace medical training → e.g. a surgeon must be qualified before anything else.
- Qualifications are the entry ticket → employers filter CVs by degree → e.g. many multinational firms in Hanoi require a bachelor's degree just to apply.

> Gợi ý: hướng "partly agree": bằng cấp mở cửa, kỹ năng xã hội giúp đi xa; tuỳ ngành.

### Hài lòng với công việc hay ổn định

Đề (18/09/2026, Discussion): Some people think job satisfaction is more important than job security; others disagree. Discuss both views and give your opinion.

**Hài lòng quan trọng hơn**
- Motivation and productivity → people who enjoy their work put in more effort → e.g. a teacher who loves her subject prepares more engaging lessons.
- Mental health → years in a job you hate cause stress and burnout → e.g. office workers with long hours and no purpose often quit within two years.
- Security is no longer guaranteed → even "safe" jobs can disappear → e.g. bank tellers replaced by mobile banking apps.

**Ổn định quan trọng hơn**
- Financial responsibilities → people with children or loans cannot take risks → e.g. a father paying a mortgage cannot afford months without income.
- Peace of mind → a predictable salary reduces anxiety → e.g. civil servants can plan long-term because their contracts are secure.

> Gợi ý: tuỳ giai đoạn đời: lúc trẻ ưu tiên hài lòng, khi có gia đình ưu tiên ổn định.

### Thà thất nghiệp còn hơn làm việc mình không thích

Đề (04/07/2026, Opinion): Many people would rather be unemployed than do a job they do not enjoy. Agree or disagree?

**Ủng hộ (hiểu được lý do)**
- Some can afford to wait → young people supported by parents prefer to search for the right job → e.g. a graduate living at home may turn down a low-paid sales role.
- Long-term thinking → a wrong first job can waste years → e.g. accepting any job may keep someone in a field they will leave anyway.

**Phản đối**
- Unemployment has serious costs → loss of income, confidence and skills → e.g. gaps on a CV make employers suspicious.
- Any job builds experience → even boring work teaches discipline and teamwork → e.g. a waiter learns customer service that helps later in hotel management.
- Enjoyment often grows over time → people learn to like jobs as they become skilled.

### Nghỉ phép cho bố

Đề (19/06 & 30/06/2026, Opinion): All fathers should be entitled to paternity leave from work. Agree or disagree?

**Ủng hộ**
- Supports mothers → the first weeks after birth are physically exhausting → e.g. a mother recovering from a caesarean needs help at home.
- Bonding with the child → fathers who are present early stay more involved later → e.g. studies in Sweden link longer leave to more active fathers.
- Gender equality at work → if only mothers take leave, employers prefer hiring men → e.g. paid leave for both parents reduces this bias.

**Phản đối**
- Cost for small businesses → a missing worker is hard to replace → e.g. a family restaurant with five staff may struggle for weeks.
- Leave may not be used → some fathers feel pressure to return early → e.g. men fear being seen as less committed.

> Gợi ý: đồng ý, nhưng đề xuất nhà nước chi trả một phần để giảm gánh nặng cho doanh nghiệp nhỏ.

### Theo đuổi đam mê hay chọn nghề thực tế

Đề (17/07/2026, Discussion): Some say young people should follow their dream job; others say they should choose a more practical career. Discuss both views.

**Theo đam mê**
- Passion leads to excellence → people practise harder at what they love → e.g. many successful chefs started cooking as a hobby.
- Life is long → forty years in an unwanted job causes regret.

**Chọn nghề thực tế**
- Job market reality → dream jobs like acting or professional gaming have very few openings → e.g. thousands compete for a handful of roles.
- Financial independence first → a stable job can fund a passion part-time → e.g. an accountant who plays in a band at weekends.

### Làm từ xa và tự động hoá (đề quen thuộc)

Đề (quen thuộc, Adv–Disadv): More and more people work from home. Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?

**Lợi**
- No commuting → saves time and money and cuts traffic → e.g. two hours a day saved for someone living on the edge of Hanoi.
- Flexibility → parents can arrange work around school hours.

**Hại**
- Isolation → fewer chances to learn from colleagues → e.g. new graduates miss informal advice in the office.
- Blurred boundaries → people answer emails late at night and burn out.

## Speaking dự đoán

**What do you do? (Part 1 – Work)**

I work as a **project coordinator** for a small logistics company in Hanoi. Basically, I **keep track of** deliveries and make sure our clients get updates on time. It's quite **fast-paced**, especially before Tết, when orders double, but I like the fact that no two days are the same.

**Why did you choose this job? (Part 1 – Work)**

To be honest, it was partly **by chance**. A friend told me about the vacancy just after I graduated, and I thought it would be a good way to **get my foot in the door**. Over time I realised I'm actually good at organising things, so I **stuck with it**.

**Would you like to change your job in the future? (Part 1 – Work)**

Possibly, yes. I'm happy where I am for now, but in a few years I'd like to **move into** a role with more responsibility, maybe managing a team. I don't think I'd **switch careers** completely, though; I'd rather **build on** the experience I already have.

**Is working in a team better than working alone? (Part 3 – Teamwork)**

I think it **depends on the task**. For creative work or big projects, a team is better because people **bounce ideas off each other** and spot mistakes you'd miss on your own. But for work that needs deep concentration, like writing a report, being alone is often more efficient. The ideal is probably a mix of both.

**What makes a good team leader? (Part 3 – Teamwork)**

A good leader, in my view, **leads by example**. If the manager stays late when there's a deadline, the team is much more willing to do the same. They also need to **give clear instructions** and listen to feedback rather than just giving orders. My first boss was like that, and people **went the extra mile** for her.

**Is politeness important at work? (Part 1 – Politeness)**

Absolutely. Small things like saying thank you or replying to emails promptly **make a big difference** to the atmosphere in an office. If colleagues are rude, people stop sharing information and **tension builds up**. I'd say politeness is the **bare minimum** for working well together.
