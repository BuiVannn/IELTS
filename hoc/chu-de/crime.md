## Cụm từ

- **commit a crime** — phạm tội — Most people who commit a crime for the first time are young and unemployed.
- **serve a prison sentence** — chịu án tù — After he served a prison sentence for theft, he struggled to find a job.
- **the reoffending rate** — tỉ lệ tái phạm — Countries that invest in prison education usually see the reoffending rate fall.
- **rehabilitation programmes** — chương trình cải tạo — Rehabilitation programmes teach prisoners skills such as cooking, welding and IT.
- **act as a deterrent** — có tác dụng răn đe — Speed cameras act as a deterrent because drivers know they will be caught.
- **juvenile delinquency** — tình trạng phạm pháp ở vị thành niên — Juvenile delinquency is often linked to school dropout and broken families.
- **turn to crime** — sa vào con đường phạm tội — Some teenagers turn to crime simply because they have nothing to do after school.
- **the root causes of crime** — nguyên nhân gốc rễ của tội phạm — Poverty and a lack of education are the root causes of crime in many areas.
- **a criminal record** — tiền án — Having a criminal record makes it almost impossible to work in a bank.
- **community service** — lao động công ích — Young offenders could do community service, such as cleaning parks, instead of going to prison.
- **online fraud** — lừa đảo trực tuyến — Online fraud targeting elderly people has risen sharply in Vietnam.
- **fall victim to** — trở thành nạn nhân của — Elderly people often fall victim to phone scams and can lose their life savings in minutes.
- **hack into** — xâm nhập trái phép (hệ thống) — Criminals hacked into the company's database and stole customers' card details.
- **run a red light** — vượt đèn đỏ — Many motorbike riders run a red light when there is no police officer around.
- **drink-driving** — lái xe sau khi uống rượu bia — Since the zero-alcohol rule came in, drink-driving has fallen noticeably in Vietnam.
- **law-abiding citizens** — công dân tuân thủ pháp luật — Law-abiding citizens should not have to live in fear of burglary.
- **harsher sentences** — mức án nặng hơn — Harsher sentences alone will not stop people who commit crimes out of desperation.
- **the death penalty** — án tử hình — Supporters of the death penalty argue that it protects society from the most dangerous criminals.
- **give offenders a second chance** — cho người phạm tội cơ hội làm lại — Job training in prison can give offenders a second chance to rebuild their lives.

## Kho ý tưởng Task 2

### Tù giam hay giáo dục để giảm tội phạm

Đề (15/03/2026, Opinion): Prison is the most common punishment, but better education is a more effective way to reduce crime. Agree or disagree?

**Đồng ý**
- Tackles the root causes of crime → education leads to stable jobs, so people have less reason to steal → e.g. a large share of prisoners in many countries left school early.
- Prison can make things worse → inmates learn from more experienced criminals → the reoffending rate stays high.
- Rehabilitation programmes in prison are also a form of education → e.g. Nordic prisons focus on training and report low reoffending.

**Không đồng ý (một phần)**
- Prison protects society from violent criminals → education cannot help in the short term.
- Some crimes are committed by well-educated people → e.g. fraud and corruption by managers.

> Gợi ý: đồng ý phần lớn, nhưng tù giam vẫn cần cho tội nghiêm trọng.

### Hình phạt nặng hơn là cách duy nhất tăng an toàn giao thông

Đề (13/06/2026, Opinion): Stricter punishments for driving offences are the only way to improve road safety. Agree or disagree?

**Đồng ý**
- Punishments act as a deterrent → e.g. after Vietnam sharply raised fines for drink-driving, many people started taking taxis home after parties.
- Losing a licence is a strong warning → drivers think twice before speeding.

**Không đồng ý**
- "Only way" is too strong → education and better infrastructure also matter → e.g. clear road markings and separate lanes for motorbikes.
- Technology → speed cameras and automatic number plate recognition catch offenders without more police.
- Penalties only work if they are enforced consistently.

### Thi lại bằng lái hằng năm

Đề (06/05/2026, Opinion): Drivers should be required to take a driving test every year. Agree or disagree?

**Đồng ý**
- Skills and health change → eyesight and reaction time decline with age → e.g. elderly drivers may not notice that they react more slowly.
- Keeps drivers up to date with new road rules.

**Không đồng ý**
- Huge cost and time for millions of drivers and test centres.
- Most accidents are caused by behaviour, not skill → people who run a red light know the rules but ignore them.
- Better alternatives → tests every 5–10 years, or annual checks only for drivers over 70 or those with offences.

### Tội phạm thanh thiếu niên

Đề (đề quen thuộc, Cause–Solution): Crime committed by young people is increasing in many countries. Why? What can be done?

**Nguyên nhân**
- Peer pressure and boredom → teenagers with no hobbies may turn to crime to impress friends.
- Family problems → parents work long hours or divorce, so children lack supervision.
- Violent content online makes aggression seem normal.

**Giải pháp**
- More youth clubs, sports and part-time job schemes → e.g. free football pitches in poorer districts.
- Community service instead of prison → avoids a criminal record and gives offenders a second chance.
- Parenting support and school counsellors who spot problems early.

### Tội phạm mạng

Đề (đề quen thuộc, Cause–Solution): Cybercrime is becoming more common. Why? How can individuals and governments deal with it?

**Nguyên nhân**
- More of life is online → banking, shopping and ID documents are all digital.
- Low risk for criminals → they can work from another country and are hard to trace.
- Weak awareness → e.g. elderly people fall victim to fake calls from "police officers" asking for bank codes.

**Giải pháp**
- Public education campaigns on TV and at banks about common scams.
- Banks should use two-factor authentication and delay large transfers to new accounts.
- International cooperation between police forces to catch gangs abroad.

## Speaking dự đoán

**Which is more effective: punishment or reward to make people obey laws? (Part 3 – Law)**

I'd say punishment is generally more effective, at least in the short term. People tend to **take rules seriously** only when there's **a real cost** to breaking them, like a large fine. Rewards can work for **minor things**, for example giving insurance discounts to drivers with **a clean record**. But for serious offences, I don't think a reward would **make much difference**.

**What new law would you like to introduce in your country? (Part 3 – Law)**

I'd like to see the ban on selling alcohol to under-18s **strictly enforced**, because at the moment it mostly exists on paper. Teenagers can **easily get hold of** beer in small corner shops. **Hefty fines** for shop owners would protect young people and probably **cut down on** road accidents as well.

**Have you ever found something someone else lost? (Part 1 – Lost & found)**

Yes, once I found a wallet on a bus with **a fair amount of cash** and an ID card inside. I **handed it in** to the bus company, and the owner called me the next day to **thank me in person**. It felt good, and I think most people in that situation would **do the right thing**.

**What do you do when you lose something? (Part 1 – Lost & found)**

First I try to **retrace my steps** and check every place I've been that day. If it's something valuable, like my phone, I **report it** to the police or the shop straight away and **block my bank cards** if needed. These days, thankfully, apps can **track down** a lost phone quite quickly.

**Are you a driver or a passenger? (Part 1 – Cars/Driving)**

I'm mostly a passenger because I don't have **a driving licence** for a car yet, only for a motorbike. To be honest, I find driving in Hanoi quite stressful since people **cut in** all the time and some **ignore the traffic lights**. I'd like to **learn to drive** properly next year, mainly for family trips.
