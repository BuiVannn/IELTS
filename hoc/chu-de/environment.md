## Cụm từ

- **carbon emissions** — lượng khí thải carbon — Aviation accounts for a growing share of global carbon emissions.
- **reduce our carbon footprint** — giảm dấu chân carbon — Taking the bus instead of driving is a simple way to reduce our carbon footprint.
- **fossil fuels** — nhiên liệu hoá thạch — Vietnam still relies heavily on fossil fuels such as coal to generate electricity.
- **renewable energy** — năng lượng tái tạo — Ninh Thuận has become a centre for renewable energy thanks to its solar farms.
- **tackle climate change** — giải quyết biến đổi khí hậu — No single country can tackle climate change on its own.
- **extreme weather events** — hiện tượng thời tiết cực đoan — Extreme weather events like Typhoon Yagi caused billions of dollars in damage.
- **single-use plastics** — nhựa dùng một lần — Many cafés in Hanoi have replaced single-use plastics with paper or bamboo straws.
- **sort household waste** — phân loại rác sinh hoạt — Residents are now required to sort household waste into three types.
- **end up in landfill** — cuối cùng nằm ở bãi chôn lấp — Most plastic bags end up in landfill or in rivers.
- **raise public awareness** — nâng cao nhận thức cộng đồng — TV campaigns can raise public awareness of how long plastic takes to break down.
- **food miles** — quãng đường thực phẩm được vận chuyển — Grapes flown in from Chile have far more food miles than locally grown dragon fruit.
- **out-of-season produce** — nông sản trái mùa — Supermarkets import out-of-season produce so that customers can buy strawberries all year.
- **support local farmers** — ủng hộ nông dân địa phương — Buying vegetables at the morning market helps support local farmers.
- **a fuel tax** — thuế nhiên liệu — A fuel tax would push people towards public transport, but it hits poor drivers hardest.
- **non-essential flights** — chuyến bay không thiết yếu — Some argue that non-essential flights, such as weekend city breaks, should be discouraged.
- **environmentally friendly** — thân thiện với môi trường — Electric motorbikes are more environmentally friendly than petrol ones.
- **adapt to rising sea levels** — thích nghi với mực nước biển dâng — Farmers in the Mekong Delta are learning to adapt to rising sea levels by switching to salt-tolerant rice.
- **take responsibility for** — chịu trách nhiệm về — Large companies should take responsibility for the packaging they produce.
- **cut down on** — cắt giảm — We could all cut down on meat and plastic without much effort.
- **deposit return scheme** — chương trình hoàn tiền khi trả vỏ chai — Under a deposit return scheme, shoppers get a small refund for every bottle they bring back.

## Kho ý tưởng Task 2

### Hạn chế bay không thiết yếu thay vì hạn chế ô tô

Đề (03/08 & 30/08/2026, Opinion): Long-distance flights pollute heavily, so non-essential flights such as tourism should be discouraged rather than limiting car use. Agree or disagree?

**Đồng ý**
- Flights pollute far more per trip → one long-haul return flight can produce as much CO2 as several months of driving → e.g. a return flight from Hanoi to London emits roughly two tonnes of CO2 per passenger.
- Air travel is used by a wealthy minority → limiting it affects fewer people than restricting cars → e.g. most people fly only once or twice a year, if at all.
- Alternatives exist for short trips → trains and coaches can replace domestic flights → e.g. France has banned some short flights where a train takes under 2.5 hours.

**Không đồng ý**
- Cars are used every day by millions → their total emissions are larger → e.g. road transport produces several times more emissions than aviation globally.
- Tourism supports jobs → many economies depend on foreign visitors → e.g. tourism makes up a large share of income in places like Hội An or Phú Quốc.
- Better to do both → cleaner fuels for planes and better public transport for cities.

### Mua thực phẩm nhập khẩu thay vì địa phương

Đề (05/08/2026; 14/06; 04/01, Cause–Solution): People buy imported rather than local food. Why? How can people be encouraged to buy local?

**Nguyên nhân**
- Perceived quality and safety → imported food is seen as cleaner → e.g. Australian beef or Japanese fruit is trusted more than local products.
- Variety all year round → out-of-season produce is available in supermarkets → e.g. apples and grapes in summer.
- Status → imported goods are given as gifts to show respect.

**Giải pháp**
- Food safety certification for local farms → e.g. VietGAP labels that buyers can check with a QR code.
- Farmers' markets and short supply chains → fresher food at fair prices.
- Campaigns on food miles → show the environmental cost of flying food across the world.

### Thực phẩm vận chuyển toàn cầu

Đề (07/08/2026; 02/07, Positive–Negative): Food is transported around the world, which gives consumers more choice. Is this a positive or negative development?

**Tích cực**
- More choice and better diets → e.g. people in cold countries can eat fresh fruit in winter.
- Export income for farmers → e.g. Vietnamese durian and dragon fruit exports bring billions of dollars to farmers.

**Tiêu cực**
- High food miles and emissions → refrigerated ships and cargo planes burn fossil fuels.
- Local farmers lose out → cheap imports undercut small producers.
- Lower freshness → food is picked early and treated with chemicals to survive long journeys.

### Tái chế chưa đủ

Đề (25/06/2026, Cause–Solution): Not enough paper, glass and cans are recycled. Why? What are the solutions?

**Nguyên nhân**
- Inconvenience → few recycling bins, no doorstep collection → e.g. in many apartment blocks all rubbish goes down one chute.
- Lack of awareness → people do not know what can be recycled.
- No financial incentive → throwing things away costs nothing.

**Giải pháp**
- Deposit return scheme → e.g. a refund of 1,000 dong per bottle, as in several European countries.
- Compulsory waste sorting with fines → Vietnam's environmental law requires households to sort household waste.
- Producer responsibility → manufacturers pay for collecting their packaging.

### Tăng giá nhiên liệu là cách tốt nhất

Đề (10/05/2026; 02/01; 26/01, Opinion): The best way to solve environmental problems is to increase the price of fuel. Agree or disagree?

**Đồng ý (một phần)**
- Price changes behaviour quickly → people drive less and share rides → e.g. when petrol prices rose sharply in 2022, many commuters switched to buses.
- Tax revenue can fund green projects → e.g. using fuel tax to build metro lines.

**Không đồng ý**
- Unfair to the poor → delivery drivers and farmers depend on fuel and cannot easily switch → e.g. a Grab driver's income falls directly.
- Raises the cost of everything → transport costs push up food prices and inflation.
- Not the only cause → plastic waste, deforestation and industrial pollution need other solutions such as regulations and renewable energy.

> Gợi ý: "best way" là từ khoá cực đoan: nên disagree một phần, đề xuất kết hợp nhiều biện pháp.

### Thích nghi thay vì ngăn chặn biến đổi khí hậu

Đề (30/05/2026, Opinion): Instead of preventing climate change, we should learn to live with it. Agree or disagree?

**Đồng ý (một phần)**
- Some change is already happening → adaptation is urgent → e.g. higher dykes and salt-tolerant rice in the Mekong Delta.

**Không đồng ý**
- Adaptation has limits → some places will become uninhabitable → e.g. low-lying islands may disappear under rising seas.
- Prevention is cheaper in the long run → every tonne of carbon avoided reduces future disaster costs.
- Poor countries suffer most → they cannot afford expensive adaptation projects.

## Speaking dự đoán

**Do you recycle? (Part 1 – Rubbish & recycling)**

I try to, yes. At home we **keep plastic bottles and cardboard** separately, and every couple of weeks my mum sells them to a scrap collector who comes round on a bicycle. It's not a perfect system, but at least those things **don't end up in landfill**. I'd be happy to do more if there were proper recycling bins in my building.

**Is recycling popular in your country? (Part 1 – Rubbish & recycling)**

It's **becoming more common**, but we still have a long way to go. In big cities like Hanoi, there's a new rule that households have to **sort their waste** into different types. The problem is that many people **aren't used to it yet**, and sometimes the separated rubbish gets mixed together in the truck anyway. So people are a bit **sceptical** about whether it makes a difference.

**How can we save paper? (Part 1 – Paper)**

The easiest way is simply to **go paperless** wherever possible, like getting bills and tickets on your phone. At work, we used to print every document, but now we **share files online** instead. Also, **printing on both sides** sounds small, but it cuts paper use almost in half.

**Who is more responsible for the environment: governments or individuals? (Part 3 – Media/Law)**

I'd say governments **bear the main responsibility**, because they have the power to change things on a large scale. For example, one law **banning single-use plastic bags** would have more impact than millions of people trying to remember their shopping bags. That said, individuals still **play a part**; if people don't follow the rules, even the best policy won't work.

**Why is ocean pollution hard to solve? (Part 3 – Place)**

Mainly because the ocean **doesn't belong to any one country**, so nobody feels fully responsible for it. Plastic thrown into a river in one country can **wash up on a beach** thousands of kilometres away. On top of that, cleaning up the sea is **incredibly expensive**, so most experts say we need to **stop the problem at the source** by producing less plastic.

**Should people be punished or rewarded to make them follow environmental laws? (Part 3 – Law)**

I think you need **a bit of both**. Fines work well for serious things like factories dumping waste into rivers, because the **penalty has to hurt**. But for everyday habits like recycling, rewards are more effective. For instance, if supermarkets **give a small discount** to people who bring their own bags, most customers will happily do it.
