## Cụm từ

- **traffic congestion** — tắc nghẽn giao thông — Traffic congestion in Ho Chi Minh City costs the economy billions of dollars a year.
- **rush hour** — giờ cao điểm — During rush hour, a 5-kilometre journey can take more than 45 minutes.
- **public transport network** — mạng lưới giao thông công cộng — Hanoi is slowly expanding its public transport network with new metro lines.
- **commute to work** — đi làm hằng ngày — Most office workers in Hanoi commute to work by motorbike.
- **a reliable bus service** — dịch vụ xe buýt đáng tin cậy — People will only leave their cars at home if there is a reliable bus service.
- **private vehicles** — phương tiện cá nhân — The city plans to restrict private vehicles in the old quarter.
- **ease congestion** — giảm ùn tắc — Building more flyovers may ease congestion in the short term, but traffic soon returns.
- **overcrowded cities** — thành phố quá đông dân — Overcrowded cities struggle to provide enough schools and hospitals.
- **migrate to urban areas** — di cư lên thành thị — Every year, thousands of young people migrate to urban areas in search of jobs.
- **urban sprawl** — đô thị phát triển tràn lan — Urban sprawl has turned rice fields on the edge of Hanoi into housing estates.
- **high-rise apartment blocks** — chung cư cao tầng — High-rise apartment blocks allow more people to live on less land.
- **green spaces** — không gian xanh — Residents need more green spaces where children can play safely.
- **noise pollution** — ô nhiễm tiếng ồn — Constant horns and construction work make noise pollution a serious problem.
- **road safety** — an toàn giao thông — Wearing a helmet is the simplest way to improve road safety for motorcyclists.
- **reckless driving** — lái xe ẩu — Reckless driving, such as running red lights, causes many accidents at night.
- **enforce the law** — thực thi pháp luật — Traffic cameras help the police enforce the law even when no officer is present.
- **heavy fines** — mức phạt nặng — Since heavy fines for drink-driving were introduced, the number of drunk drivers has fallen.
- **pedestrian-friendly** — thân thiện với người đi bộ — The walking streets around Hoàn Kiếm Lake make the area much more pedestrian-friendly.
- **cost of living** — chi phí sinh hoạt — The high cost of living pushes young families out of the city centre.
- **decentralise jobs and services** — phân tán việc làm và dịch vụ — To reduce pressure on the capital, the government could decentralise jobs and services to smaller cities.

## Kho ý tưởng Task 2

### Giao thông công cộng miễn phí 24/7

Đề (13/06/2026; 19/04, Opinion): The best way to reduce traffic congestion is to provide free public transport 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. Agree or disagree?

**Đồng ý**
- Price matters → free fares give people a strong reason to leave their private vehicles at home → e.g. Luxembourg made all public transport free in 2020.
- Helps low-income workers → night-shift workers can travel safely at any hour.

**Không đồng ý**
- Price is not the main problem → buses are slow, crowded and do not reach every neighbourhood → e.g. many Hanoi residents ride motorbikes because buses take twice as long, not because tickets cost 7,000 dong.
- Huge cost → running services all night with few passengers wastes taxpayers' money.
- Better solutions → more metro lines, bus lanes, congestion charges and flexible working hours.

> Gợi ý: "the best way" → disagree; luận điểm chính: chất lượng và độ phủ quan trọng hơn giá vé.

### Siêu đô thị trên 20 triệu dân

Đề (07/02/2026, Positive–Negative): More and more people live in mega-cities with over 20 million inhabitants. Is this a positive or negative development?

**Tích cực**
- Jobs and higher incomes → companies and investment concentrate in big cities → e.g. Ho Chi Minh City produces about a fifth of Vietnam's GDP.
- Better services → top hospitals, universities and entertainment in one place.
- Efficient use of land → high-rise apartment blocks house more people per square kilometre.

**Tiêu cực**
- Traffic congestion and pollution → e.g. Jakarta and Manila are known for some of the worst traffic in Asia.
- Housing costs → rent takes a huge share of income.
- Pressure on infrastructure → flooding, water shortages and overloaded hospitals.

### Thành phố quá lớn, quá đông

Đề (04/04/2026, Cause–Solution): Major cities have become too big and overcrowded. Why? What are the solutions?

**Nguyên nhân**
- Rural–urban migration → people migrate to urban areas for jobs and education → e.g. young people from the Mekong Delta moving to Bình Dương factories.
- Concentration of services → the best schools and hospitals are only in big cities.

**Giải pháp**
- Develop satellite towns and smaller cities → decentralise jobs and services → e.g. moving universities and government offices to the suburbs.
- Invest in rural areas → better roads, internet and jobs so people can stay.
- Better urban planning → metro lines connecting the centre with new residential areas.

### Nâng tuổi được lái xe để tăng an toàn

Đề (24/06/2026, Opinion): Raising the minimum driving age is the best way to improve road safety. Agree or disagree?

**Đồng ý (một phần)**
- Young drivers take more risks → statistics in many countries show drivers under 21 are more likely to crash.
- More maturity → older learners make better judgements.

**Không đồng ý**
- Experience matters more than age → a new driver of 25 is still inexperienced.
- Other causes → drink-driving, speeding and using phones affect all ages.
- Better measures → stricter driving tests, probation periods, heavy fines and traffic cameras → e.g. Vietnam's 2020 drink-driving fines led to a clear drop in accidents.

### Vì sao người ta vẫn vi phạm luật giao thông

Đề (S9, 06/2026, Cause–Solution): Some people still break driving laws such as speeding or using phones. Why? What measures can help?

**Nguyên nhân**
- Low chance of being caught → few cameras and police on minor roads.
- Overconfidence and habit → people believe "it won't happen to me".
- Time pressure → delivery drivers paid per trip drive fast and check apps while riding.

**Giải pháp**
- Enforce the law with cameras and automatic fines sent to the owner.
- Penalty points → repeat offenders lose their licence.
- Education → road safety lessons at school and shocking real-life campaigns.

### Ngày không ô tô ở trung tâm

Đề (25/01/2026, Adv–Disadv): Some cities have car-free days in the city centre. Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?

**Lợi ích**
- Cleaner air and less noise for a day → raises public awareness of pollution.
- Pedestrian-friendly streets → families walk, cycle and use local shops → e.g. Hanoi's weekend walking streets bring crowds to the old quarter.

**Bất lợi**
- Disruption → deliveries, taxis and emergency services face difficulties.
- Only symbolic → one day a month changes little unless public transport improves.

## Speaking dự đoán

**Do you like travelling by car? (Part 1 – Cars/Driving)**

Not especially, to be honest. In Hanoi, cars are **painfully slow** at rush hour, and finding a parking space is **a nightmare**. I usually ride my motorbike because I can **weave through traffic** much more easily. That said, on long trips with my family, a car is definitely **more comfortable**, especially in the rainy season.

**What do you do in a traffic jam? (Part 1 – Cars/Driving)**

Usually I just **try to stay calm** and listen to a podcast, because there's not much else you can do. If I'm on my motorbike, I sometimes **take a side street** to get around it. What I really **can't stand** is people honking their horns non-stop, as if that will make the traffic move.

**Should there be more parks in your city? (Part 1 – Parks)**

Absolutely. Hanoi has a few big parks, but in the newer districts there are hardly any **green spaces** at all, just rows of apartment blocks. Parks give people somewhere to **get some fresh air** and exercise for free. I think every new housing project should be **required by law** to include one.

**How can cities reduce traffic congestion? (Part 3 – Experience)**

I think the most effective long-term solution is a **fast and reliable public transport network**, especially metro lines, because they don't get stuck in traffic. In the short term, cities could encourage **flexible working hours** so that not everyone is on the road at 8 am. Another option is a **congestion charge** for driving into the city centre, as London does.

**Would free public transport solve the problem? (Part 3 – Experience)**

Only **partly**, in my opinion. In Vietnam, bus tickets are **already very cheap**, yet most people still prefer their motorbikes because buses are slow and don't **stop close to their homes**. So I think making it free wouldn't change much on its own. What really needs to improve is **frequency and coverage**.

**Why are buildings getting taller? (Part 3 – Place)**

Mainly because **land in city centres** is so expensive and limited. Building upwards is the only way to **fit more people** into the same area. There's also an element of **prestige**: cities like to show off with **iconic skyscrapers**, like Landmark 81 in Ho Chi Minh City, which has become a symbol of the city.
