## Cụm từ

- **a well-rounded education** — nền giáo dục toàn diện — Art and sport are part of a well-rounded education, not optional extras.
- **rote learning** — học vẹt, học thuộc lòng — Vietnamese students spend hours on rote learning before university entrance exams.
- **develop critical thinking** — phát triển tư duy phản biện — Debating in class helps teenagers develop critical thinking far better than copying model answers.
- **tuition fees** — học phí — Tuition fees at many private universities in Hanoi now exceed 50 million dong a year.
- **access to higher education** — cơ hội học đại học — Scholarships widen access to higher education for students from rural provinces.
- **a heavy academic workload** — khối lượng học nặng — With a heavy academic workload, many pupils have no time left for hobbies.
- **cram for an exam** — học dồn trước kỳ thi — I used to cram for an exam the night before, and I forgot everything a week later.
- **practical skills** — kỹ năng thực tế — Employers complain that graduates lack practical skills such as writing a clear email.
- **job-ready graduates** — sinh viên ra trường làm được việc ngay — Companies want job-ready graduates who need little extra training.
- **a shortage of qualified teachers** — thiếu giáo viên có chuyên môn — Remote areas suffer from a shortage of qualified teachers in subjects like physics.
- **lose concentration** — mất tập trung — Children tend to lose concentration after about twenty minutes of listening to a lecture.
- **a short attention span** — khả năng tập trung ngắn — Short videos may be giving young people a short attention span.
- **learn at their own pace** — học theo tốc độ của mình — Online courses let adults learn at their own pace after work.
- **face-to-face interaction** — tương tác trực tiếp — Schools offer face-to-face interaction that no app can fully replace.
- **be a financial burden** — là gánh nặng tài chính — University fees can be a financial burden on low-income families.
- **lifelong learning** — học tập suốt đời — In a fast-changing job market, lifelong learning is no longer a choice.
- **foster creativity** — nuôi dưỡng sự sáng tạo — Drawing and music lessons foster creativity in ways that maths drills cannot.
- **basic literacy and numeracy** — biết đọc viết và tính toán cơ bản — Adults without basic literacy and numeracy struggle to fill in a simple bank form.
- **a solid foundation** — nền tảng vững chắc — Primary school should give every child a solid foundation in reading and maths.

## Kho ý tưởng Task 2

### Môn nghệ thuật bắt buộc ở cấp 3

Đề (04/09 & 16/09/2026, Opinion): Art classes such as painting and drawing are as important as other subjects, so they should be compulsory in high school. Agree or disagree?

**Ủng hộ**
- Art fosters creativity → creative thinking is needed in design, marketing and even engineering → e.g. product designers at a phone company start with hand sketches, not equations.
- Art reduces stress → students under exam pressure need a calm, non-graded activity → e.g. a weekly 45-minute painting class gives Grade 12 students a break from mock exams.
- Art builds cultural awareness → students learn about local traditions and heritage → e.g. a class project on Đông Hồ folk paintings teaches history through practice.

**Phản đối**
- Limited timetable → every hour of art is an hour taken from maths, science or English → e.g. Vietnamese high school students already study 12–13 subjects.
- Not every student has talent or interest → forcing them may cause frustration rather than creativity → e.g. a student who is weak at drawing may feel embarrassed in front of classmates.
- Better as an elective → students choose what suits their goals → e.g. an art club after school serves the passionate ones without burdening everyone.

> Gợi ý: hướng an toàn là "partly agree": bắt buộc ở cấp 2, tự chọn ở cấp 3.

### Ít học sinh chọn khối khoa học

Đề (23/08/2026; 16/06/2026, Cause–Effect): In many countries not enough students choose science subjects. What are the causes? What will be the effects on society?

**Nguyên nhân**
- Science is seen as difficult → heavy maths and long hours in the lab put students off → e.g. many pupils drop physics as soon as they can.
- Poor career image → science jobs look less well paid than business or IT → e.g. a young researcher may earn far less than a sales manager of the same age.
- Lack of qualified teachers and equipment → lessons become theory-only → e.g. rural schools may have no working laboratory at all.

**Hậu quả**
- Shortage of doctors, engineers and researchers → countries depend on foreign experts → e.g. a new metro line may rely on overseas engineering firms.
- Slower innovation → fewer local inventions and patents → e.g. the country imports technology instead of developing its own.
- Weaker public understanding of science → people believe false health claims online → e.g. rumours about vaccines spread quickly on social media.

### Trường học dạy kỹ năng nghề hay môn truyền thống

Đề (19/08/2026, Opinion): Schools should focus on employment skills rather than traditional subjects. Agree or disagree?

**Ủng hộ**
- Graduates need practical skills → employers want people who can communicate, use software and work in teams → e.g. many companies run three-month training courses because new staff cannot write a basic report.
- Reduces youth unemployment → job-ready graduates find work faster → e.g. vocational high schools in Germany place most students directly into apprenticeships.

**Phản đối**
- Traditional subjects build a solid foundation → maths and literature train logic and expression, which every job needs → e.g. a data analyst relies on school-level statistics.
- Jobs change quickly → specific skills become outdated, general knowledge lasts → e.g. software taught in 2020 may already be obsolete.
- School is not only for work → it also prepares citizens → e.g. history and civics help young people understand the society they vote in.

> Gợi ý: kết luận cân bằng: giữ môn truyền thống làm nền, lồng kỹ năng nghề vào (dự án, thuyết trình).

### Internet thay thế trường học?

Đề (28/07/2026, Opinion): Schools are no longer necessary because children can learn everything from the Internet at home. Agree or disagree?

**Ủng hộ (ý yếu, dùng để nhượng bộ)**
- Online content is huge and free → children can learn at their own pace → e.g. a student can watch a chemistry lesson three times until it makes sense.

**Phản đối**
- Children need guidance → young learners cannot judge which information is reliable → e.g. a ten-year-old searching "history of Vietnam" may land on inaccurate videos.
- Schools teach social skills → face-to-face interaction, teamwork and conflict resolution happen in class and in the playground → e.g. group projects teach children how to share work fairly.
- Discipline and structure → most children lose concentration at home without a timetable → e.g. during the 2021 school closures many pupils fell behind.
- Schools provide childcare and equality → poor families may lack devices or a stable connection → e.g. one laptop shared by three siblings.

### Đại học miễn phí hay sinh viên tự trả

Đề (13/06/2026; 10/05; 24/02, Discussion): Some think governments should provide free education at all levels; others think students should pay for university. Discuss both views.

**Miễn phí**
- Equal opportunity → talent should matter more than family income → e.g. a bright student from Hà Giang should not miss university because of fees.
- Benefits the whole society → graduates pay more tax and fill skilled jobs → e.g. more trained nurses reduce pressure on public hospitals.

**Sinh viên trả**
- Graduates benefit personally → they usually earn more over their lifetime, so it is fair that they contribute → e.g. a graduate may earn 50% more than a school leaver.
- Limited public budget → money for universities could go to primary schools or healthcare → e.g. building rural schools may help more children.
- Students who pay tend to take their studies more seriously → fewer drop out or change courses aimlessly.

> Gợi ý: hướng trung gian hay ăn điểm: học phí có hỗ trợ (student loans, scholarships) thay vì miễn phí hoàn toàn.

### Học sinh khó tập trung

Đề (07/07/2026, Cause–Solution): Many students find it hard to concentrate at school. Why? What can be done?

**Nguyên nhân**
- Smartphones and short videos → a short attention span makes 45-minute lessons feel long → e.g. checking notifications every few minutes.
- Lack of sleep → late-night gaming or extra classes → e.g. teenagers who sleep six hours a night struggle in morning lessons.
- One-way teaching → long lectures with little interaction → e.g. copying notes from the board for a whole period.

**Giải pháp**
- Phone rules → collect phones at the start of the day → e.g. several schools in Hanoi use phone lockers.
- Interactive lessons → short activities, group work, quizzes → e.g. a five-minute quiz every 15 minutes.
- Reduce the workload → fewer evening extra classes so students get enough rest.

## Speaking dự đoán

**Do you remember your primary school teachers? (Part 1 – Teachers)**

Yes, quite clearly, especially my Year 3 teacher, Ms Lan. She was **strict but fair**, and she had a habit of reading us a short story at the end of every Friday. I think she's the reason I **got into reading** at such a young age. I actually **bumped into her** at a market a couple of years ago and she still remembered my name.

**What was your favourite subject at secondary school? (Part 1 – Secondary school)**

I'd say geography. I liked it because it wasn't just about **memorising facts**; we got to look at maps and talk about why cities grow in certain places. Our teacher often linked lessons to **real-life issues** like flooding in the Mekong Delta, so it never felt abstract. Maths, on the other hand, was never **my strong point**.

**Did you like science at school? (Part 1 – Science)**

To be honest, not really at first. Chemistry seemed like a long list of formulas to **learn by heart**. That changed in Year 11 when we finally **did some experiments** in the lab, and I realised the theory actually explained what was happening in front of me. So I'd say I **warmed to it** later on.

**Online or face-to-face courses: which is better for learning? (Part 3 – Courses)**

I think it **depends on the learner** and the subject. Online courses are brilliant for busy adults because they can **learn at their own pace** and replay lessons as many times as they need. But for skills like speaking a language or playing an instrument, **face-to-face interaction** makes a huge difference, because a teacher can correct you on the spot. Personally, I find I **stay motivated** much longer in a real classroom.

**Is it easier to learn skills as a child or as an adult? (Part 3 – Skills)**

Generally speaking, children **pick things up** faster, particularly languages and sports, because they aren't afraid of making mistakes. Adults, on the other hand, are more **self-disciplined** and know exactly why they're learning something. For example, I learnt to swim at seven **without much effort**, but my father learnt at forty and it took him months. So children have the advantage in speed, while adults have the advantage in focus.

**Should children be taught about money at school? (Part 3 – Experience)**

Yes, I'm **all for it**. A lot of young people leave school without knowing how to **set a budget** or how interest on a loan works, and then they **get into debt** with buy-now-pay-later apps. Even one lesson a month in secondary school could make a real difference. It doesn't have to be complicated, just the basics of saving and spending wisely.
