## Cụm từ

- **the extended family** — đại gia đình nhiều thế hệ — In rural Vietnam, the extended family often lives under one roof.
- **the nuclear family** — gia đình hạt nhân (bố mẹ và con) — The nuclear family has become the norm in big cities like Ho Chi Minh City.
- **live independently** — sống tự lập — More graduates choose to live independently in a rented studio rather than with their parents.
- **feel isolated** — cảm thấy cô lập — Elderly people living alone in high-rise flats can easily feel isolated.
- **start a family** — lập gia đình, sinh con — Many couples now wait until their thirties to start a family.
- **delay parenthood** — trì hoãn việc làm cha mẹ — Rising house prices push young couples to delay parenthood.
- **financially secure** — vững vàng về tài chính — Parents who are financially secure can give their children more opportunities.
- **the generation gap** — khoảng cách thế hệ — The generation gap shows clearly when grandparents and teenagers discuss marriage.
- **peer pressure** — áp lực từ bạn bè đồng trang lứa — Peer pressure can push teenagers to try vaping just to fit in.
- **fit in with** — hoà nhập với — Children who move schools often struggle to fit in with their new classmates.
- **bring up children** — nuôi dạy con cái — Bringing up children in a busy city is expensive and tiring.
- **set boundaries** — đặt ra giới hạn — Parents need to set boundaries on phone use from an early age.
- **instil values in** — vun đắp giá trị cho — Grandparents often instil values such as respect and thrift in their grandchildren.
- **pass down traditions** — truyền lại truyền thống — Making bánh chưng together at Tết is a way to pass down traditions.
- **look after elderly parents** — chăm sóc cha mẹ già — In Vietnam it is still expected that sons look after elderly parents.
- **a care home** — viện dưỡng lão — Sending a parent to a care home is still seen as shameful by many Vietnamese families.
- **a close-knit family** — gia đình gắn bó — I grew up in a close-knit family where everyone ate dinner together.
- **quality time** — thời gian chất lượng bên nhau — Working parents try to spend quality time with their children at weekends.
- **role reversal** — đổi vai (con chăm sóc lại cha mẹ) — There is a kind of role reversal when adult children start caring for their parents.
- **sibling rivalry** — sự ganh đua giữa anh chị em — Sibling rivalry is common when parents compare their children's exam results.

## Kho ý tưởng Task 2

### Sống một mình hoặc gia đình nhỏ thay vì đại gia đình

Đề (09/08/2026, Positive/Negative): In cities, more people live alone or in small families rather than in extended families. Is this a positive or negative development?

**Tích cực**
- Independence and privacy → young adults learn to manage money and housework → e.g. a graduate renting a studio learns to budget for rent and bills.
- Fewer conflicts → different generations have different lifestyles → e.g. no arguments over bedtimes or what to watch on TV.
- Mobility → small households can move for jobs easily.

**Tiêu cực**
- Loneliness → elderly people living alone feel isolated → e.g. an older woman whose children work in another city.
- Childcare costs → no grandparents to help → e.g. young parents in Hanoi paying for nannies or private nurseries.
- Weaker transmission of values and traditions.

> Gợi ý: "mostly positive nhưng cần dịch vụ cộng đồng cho người già".

### Áp lực bạn bè

Đề (01/08/2026, Adv–Disadv): Peer pressure: do the disadvantages outweigh the advantages?

**Bất lợi**
- Risky behaviour → teenagers smoke, vape or race motorbikes to fit in.
- Loss of identity → following the group instead of personal interests → e.g. choosing a university major because friends chose it.
- Spending pressure → buying expensive phones or clothes to keep up.

**Lợi ích**
- Positive motivation → studying harder when friends do → e.g. a study group preparing for the university entrance exam together.
- Healthy habits → joining friends at the gym or a running club.

> Gợi ý: kết luận bất lợi lớn hơn ở tuổi teen, nhưng có thể biến thành động lực nếu chọn đúng nhóm bạn.

### Sinh con muộn hơn trước

Đề (12/07/2026, Cause + Adv–Disadv): People are having children later in life than in the past. Why? Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?

**Nguyên nhân**
- Longer education and career focus → women complete degrees and build careers first.
- Housing and childcare costs → couples want to be financially secure first → e.g. saving for years before buying a flat.

**Lợi**
- Older parents are more mature and stable → they can afford good schools and spend more patiently.

**Hại**
- Health risks and fertility problems → pregnancy after 35 carries higher risks.
- Less family support → grandparents may be too old to help with childcare → e.g. parents near retirement while their child is still at university.

### Ảnh hưởng ngoài gia đình vs gia đình

Đề (27/08/2026, Opinion): Influences outside the home, such as friends, TV and social media, have a greater effect on children's development than family. Agree or disagree?

**Đồng ý**
- Time spent → teenagers spend more hours at school and online than with parents.
- Social media sets trends → e.g. children copy language and fashion from influencers.

**Phản đối**
- Early years are formed at home → language, manners and values come from parents.
- Parents choose the environment → which school, which friends, how much screen access.

> Gợi ý: tuổi nhỏ gia đình quyết định, tuổi teen bên ngoài mạnh hơn.

### Người già và quan hệ ông bà – cháu (đề quen thuộc)

Đề (quen thuộc, Opinion): Some think the elderly should live with their families; others think care homes are better. Discuss both views.

**Sống cùng gia đình**
- Emotional support → older people feel valued and less lonely.
- Benefits for grandchildren → grandparents pass down traditions and help with childcare → e.g. a grandmother teaching cooking and folk stories.

**Viện dưỡng lão**
- Professional care → nurses handle illnesses like dementia better than busy relatives.
- Social life → residents make friends of the same age.

### Lớp học làm cha mẹ hằng năm

Đề (12/06/2026, Opinion): Parents should attend parenting courses every year to ensure a happy family life. Agree or disagree?

**Ủng hộ**
- Parenting changes → new challenges such as online safety and mental health.
- Reduces conflict → parents learn to communicate calmly with teenagers.

**Phản đối**
- Impractical every year → working parents lack time and money.
- Every family is different → a single course cannot fit all cultures and situations.

## Speaking dự đoán

**Which room does your family spend the most time in? (Part 1 – Home)**

Definitely the living room. That's where we **have meals together** every evening, since we eat on a mat on the floor in the traditional way. After dinner my dad watches the news and my mum and I **catch up on** each other's day. It's really **the heart of the house**.

**Did your parents teach you to be polite? (Part 1 – Politeness)**

Yes, very much so. In Vietnam children are taught to **greet older relatives** properly, and my mum was quite strict about that. She also made me use the right pronouns when talking to people older than me. Looking back, I'm grateful because those habits **come naturally** to me now.

**Why do some people admire older people? (Part 3 – Older people)**

I think it's mostly because older people have **been through a lot**. My grandparents, for instance, raised five children during very hard times, and they never complained. People admire that kind of **resilience**. Also, in Vietnamese culture, respecting older people is **deeply rooted**, so admiration comes quite naturally.

**What can young people learn from the elderly? (Part 3 – Older people)**

Quite a lot, actually. Older people have **life experience** that you can't get from books, for example how to handle money in hard times or how to keep a marriage going. They also tend to be more **patient** and less obsessed with material things. If young people **took the time** to listen, they could avoid many mistakes.

**Do parents or friends influence young people's opinions more? (Part 3 – Opinions)**

I'd say it **shifts with age**. Young children mostly repeat what their parents say, but once they become teenagers, friends start to **carry more weight**, especially on things like fashion and music. On serious issues such as careers or marriage, though, parents often **still have the final say** in Vietnam.

**Why do older people change their opinions less? (Part 3 – Opinions)**

Partly because their views are **based on decades of experience**, so they trust them more. They may also feel that changing their mind means admitting they were wrong for years, which is hard. And honestly, older people are less exposed to **new ideas**, since they spend less time online or meeting new people. That said, my grandfather has become much more **open-minded** since he started using Zalo.
