## Cụm từ

- **target audience** — nhóm khách hàng mục tiêu — The target audience for energy drinks is mainly teenagers and young men.
- **persuade consumers to** — thuyết phục người tiêu dùng — Clever slogans persuade consumers to buy products they had never thought about.
- **impulse buying** — mua sắm bốc đồng — Flash sales on Shopee encourage impulse buying late at night.
- **a consumer society** — xã hội tiêu dùng — In a consumer society, people often judge success by what they own.
- **create artificial needs** — tạo ra nhu cầu giả — Phone makers create artificial needs by releasing a new model every year.
- **misleading claims** — quảng cáo sai sự thật, gây hiểu lầm — Some slimming tea brands have been fined for misleading claims about weight loss.
- **celebrity endorsement** — người nổi tiếng quảng cáo cho sản phẩm — Celebrity endorsement can double the sales of a skincare brand within weeks.
- **brand loyalty** — sự trung thành với thương hiệu — Brand loyalty is strong among iPhone users, who rarely switch to Android.
- **targeted advertising** — quảng cáo nhắm mục tiêu — Targeted advertising uses your search history to show you products you might want.
- **ad-blocking software** — phần mềm chặn quảng cáo — Many students install ad-blocking software to avoid pop-ups while studying.
- **sponsored content** — nội dung được tài trợ — Sponsored content on TikTok often looks like an honest review, which makes it hard to spot.
- **a reliable source of information** — nguồn tin đáng tin cậy — Official government websites are a more reliable source of information than anonymous Facebook pages.
- **go viral** — lan truyền chóng mặt — Small cafés hope their videos will go viral and attract thousands of customers.
- **spread misinformation** — lan truyền thông tin sai lệch — During floods, some pages spread misinformation about dams breaking.
- **verify the facts** — kiểm chứng sự thật — Good journalists verify the facts before publishing a story.
- **sensationalist headlines** — tiêu đề giật gân — Sensationalist headlines get clicks but often distort what really happened.
- **in the public eye** — được công chúng chú ý — Footballers who are constantly in the public eye should behave responsibly.
- **a positive role model** — tấm gương tích cực — An athlete who studies hard and avoids scandals can be a positive role model for teenagers.
- **invade someone's privacy** — xâm phạm đời tư — Photographers who follow singers home invade their privacy.
- **media literacy** — khả năng đọc hiểu, đánh giá thông tin truyền thông — Schools should teach media literacy so that children can tell an advert from a news report.

## Kho ý tưởng Task 2

### Quảng cáo chỉ để bán thứ không cần

Đề (04/04/2026; 10/01/2026, Opinion): The main aim of advertising is to sell products that people do not really need. Agree or disagree?

**Ủng hộ**
- Ads create artificial needs → they link products to happiness or status → e.g. luxury handbag ads suggest you are not successful without one.
- Constant upgrades → companies persuade people to replace items that still work → e.g. a phone bought two years ago is presented as outdated.

**Phản đối**
- Ads inform consumers → people learn about new products, prices and offers → e.g. a supermarket leaflet showing discounts on rice and cooking oil.
- Public service advertising → some campaigns promote health and safety → e.g. road safety ads urging motorbike riders to wear helmets.
- Competition benefits buyers → advertising forces firms to improve quality and lower prices → e.g. mobile networks competing with cheaper data packages.

> Gợi ý: "disagree" an toàn hơn: mục đích là bán hàng, nhưng không chỉ là thứ không cần.

### Cấm toàn bộ quảng cáo

Đề (01/03/2026, Opinion): Society would benefit from a ban on all advertising. Agree or disagree?

**Ủng hộ**
- Less consumerism and debt → fewer temptations to overspend → e.g. buy-now-pay-later apps advertised to students.
- Cleaner public spaces → fewer billboards and pop-ups → e.g. São Paulo banned outdoor billboards in 2007.

**Phản đối**
- Free media depends on ads → newspapers, YouTube and TV are funded by advertisers → e.g. without ads, viewers would have to pay subscriptions.
- Small businesses need visibility → a new café relies on Facebook ads to find customers.
- A total ban is extreme → regulating harmful ads is enough → e.g. banning junk food ads during children's programmes.

### Người tiêu dùng ít bị quảng cáo tác động hơn

Đề (05/01/2026; 03/07/2026, Cause + Positive/Negative): Consumers are less influenced by advertising than before. Why? Is this positive or negative?

**Nguyên nhân**
- Too many ads → people become immune to them → e.g. most users skip YouTube ads after five seconds.
- Online reviews → shoppers trust other buyers more than brands → e.g. reading Shopee ratings before buying.
- Ad blockers and paid streaming → people simply see fewer ads.

**Tích cực / tiêu cực**
- Positive → people make more informed decisions and waste less money.
- Negative (nhẹ) → advertising becomes hidden → e.g. sponsored content disguised as honest reviews by influencers.

### Đọc tin tức trên mạng thay vì báo, TV

Đề (18/03/2026, Positive/Negative): More people get their news from the Internet rather than newspapers or television. Is this a positive or negative development?

**Tích cực**
- Speed → news is updated within minutes → e.g. typhoon warnings shared instantly on official pages.
- Choice and access → free news from many countries and viewpoints.

**Tiêu cực**
- Misinformation spreads quickly → anyone can publish without checking → e.g. false rumours about a celebrity's death shared thousands of times.
- Filter bubbles → algorithms show only views people already agree with.
- Clickbait → sensationalist headlines replace careful reporting.

> Gợi ý: kết luận "positive overall nếu người đọc có media literacy".

### Người nổi tiếng làm gương (đề quen thuộc)

Đề (quen thuộc, Opinion): Celebrities have a responsibility to be good role models for young people. Agree or disagree?

**Ủng hộ**
- Huge influence → teenagers copy their style and habits → e.g. fans buy the same trainers as their favourite footballer.
- Their fame comes from the public → so they owe something back, such as supporting charities.

**Phản đối**
- They are ordinary people → their job is to sing or play, not to raise children → e.g. a singer's private mistakes should not be judged forever.
- Parents and teachers are the real role models → children spend far more time with them.

## Speaking dự đoán

**Do you watch advertisements? (Part 1 – Advertisements)**

Not if I can help it, to be honest. I usually **skip** YouTube ads as soon as the button appears. That said, I do **pay attention to** ads on Facebook when they're about restaurants nearby, because they're actually useful. So I'd say I watch them **selectively**.

**Have you ever bought something because of an ad? (Part 1 – Advertisements)**

Yes, a pair of running shoes last year. The ad showed someone running along the West Lake at sunrise, and it **really appealed to me** because I'd just started jogging. In the end the shoes were fine, but I probably **paid over the odds** for the brand name.

**Do you spend too much time on social media? (Part 1 – Social media)**

Probably, yes. I tell myself I'll just check messages, and then I **lose track of time** scrolling through short videos. Recently I've turned off notifications, which has helped a bit. I'm trying to use it **in moderation** rather than quitting completely.

**Is news on social media reliable? (Part 3 – News)**

On the whole, I'd say it's **hit and miss**. Some pages are run by proper newspapers, so they're trustworthy, but a lot of posts are just rumours that people **share without thinking**. The problem is that false stories often spread faster than the corrections. So I always try to **double-check** important news on an official site.

**Do famous people in ads influence what people buy? (Part 3 – Advertising)**

Definitely, especially young people. When a popular singer **promotes** a skincare product, fans assume it must be good, even though the singer is **being paid to say so**. Older consumers tend to be more **sceptical** and look at price and quality instead. That's why companies spend so much on celebrity deals.

**Should children be protected from advertising? (Part 3 – Advertising)**

Yes, I think so. Young children **can't tell the difference** between a cartoon and an advert, so they're easy targets. Ads for sweets and toys can lead to constant **pestering** at the supermarket. I'd support **stricter rules** on advertising during children's programmes and on apps designed for kids.
