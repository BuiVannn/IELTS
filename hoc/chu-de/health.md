## Cụm từ

- **lead a sedentary lifestyle** — lối sống ít vận động — Office workers who lead a sedentary lifestyle are more likely to have back problems.
- **processed food** — thực phẩm chế biến sẵn — Instant noodles and sausages are cheap processed food that many students rely on.
- **a balanced diet** — chế độ ăn cân bằng — A balanced diet should include rice, vegetables, fish and some fruit every day.
- **skip meals** — bỏ bữa — Busy workers often skip meals and then overeat at night.
- **junk food** — đồ ăn vặt kém lành mạnh — Junk food is sold right outside many school gates in Hanoi.
- **childhood obesity** — béo phì ở trẻ em — Childhood obesity has risen sharply in Vietnamese cities over the last fifteen years.
- **keep fit** — giữ dáng, giữ sức khoẻ — My father keeps fit by playing badminton three evenings a week.
- **take regular exercise** — tập thể dục đều đặn — Doctors advise older people to take regular exercise, even if it is just walking.
- **lifestyle diseases** — bệnh do lối sống — Diabetes and heart disease are lifestyle diseases linked to diet and inactivity.
- **put a strain on the healthcare system** — gây áp lực lên hệ thống y tế — Rising obesity will put a strain on the healthcare system for decades.
- **preventive healthcare** — y tế dự phòng — Free annual check-ups are a form of preventive healthcare that saves money later.
- **extreme sports** — thể thao mạo hiểm — Extreme sports such as paragliding are becoming popular in Mù Cang Chải.
- **take calculated risks** — chấp nhận rủi ro có tính toán — Experienced climbers take calculated risks and always check the weather first.
- **freedom of choice** — quyền tự do lựa chọn — Banning dangerous sports would limit adults' freedom of choice.
- **safety regulations** — quy định an toàn — Strict safety regulations have made bungee jumping far less dangerous than it looks.
- **host a major sporting event** — đăng cai sự kiện thể thao lớn — Hosting a major sporting event like the SEA Games requires years of preparation.
- **boost national pride** — khơi dậy niềm tự hào dân tộc — Vietnam's football victories boost national pride and bring people onto the streets.
- **a huge financial gamble** — canh bạc tài chính lớn — Building new stadiums for a two-week tournament is a huge financial gamble.
- **promote grassroots sport** — phát triển thể thao phong trào — The money would be better spent to promote grassroots sport in local communities.
- **get enough sleep** — ngủ đủ giấc — Teenagers who do not get enough sleep find it harder to control their appetite.

## Kho ý tưởng Task 2

### Cấm thể thao nguy hiểm

Đề (11/07 & 16/07/2026; 04/03/2026, Discussion/Opinion): Some people think dangerous sports should be banned; others think people should have the freedom to choose. Discuss both views.

**Nên cấm**
- Risk of death and serious injury → some accidents cannot be prevented → e.g. paragliding crashes caused by sudden wind changes.
- Cost to society → rescues and hospital treatment are paid for by the public → e.g. mountain rescue teams risking their own lives.
- Bad influence on the young → teenagers copy stunts without training → e.g. motorbike stunts filmed for TikTok.

**Quyền lựa chọn**
- Adults are responsible for their own lives → the state does not ban smoking or fast driving on empty roads.
- Benefits → confidence, discipline and enjoyment → e.g. rock climbers learn to stay calm under pressure.
- Regulation works better than bans → licences, equipment checks, trained instructors → e.g. skydiving clubs require several training sessions before a first jump.

> Gợi ý: hướng an toàn: không cấm, nhưng siết quy định an toàn và cấm với trẻ vị thành niên.

### Đăng cai sự kiện thể thao quốc tế

Đề (14/08/2026; 08/03/2026, Discussion): Some say hosting international sports events is good for a country; others say it is bad. Discuss both views.

**Có lợi**
- Tourism and global image → millions watch on TV → e.g. the 2022 SEA Games brought visitors to Hanoi and neighbouring provinces.
- Better infrastructure → new roads, metro lines and stadiums remain after the event.
- National pride and interest in sport → children start playing after watching their heroes.

**Có hại**
- Huge costs and debt → budgets often run far over → e.g. Rio 2016 left several unused venues.
- Displacement and disruption → residents moved, traffic chaos during the event.
- Money taken from schools and hospitals.

### Lối sống bận rộn làm hại thói quen ăn uống

Đề (05/09/2026, Cause–Solution): Busy lifestyles are harming people's eating habits and health. What are the causes? What solutions can be suggested?

**Nguyên nhân**
- Long working hours and commutes → no time to cook → e.g. ordering fried chicken through delivery apps after a twelve-hour day.
- Cheap, convenient processed food → instant noodles cost less than a fresh meal.
- Stress → people eat for comfort, not hunger.

**Giải pháp**
- Workplaces → canteens with healthy options, proper lunch breaks.
- Education → cooking and nutrition lessons at school → e.g. teaching teenagers to cook three simple meals.
- Government → sugar tax and clear food labels → e.g. the UK sugar tax led drink makers to cut sugar.

### Béo phì và y tế công (đề quen thuộc)

Đề (quen thuộc, Opinion): Governments should be responsible for reducing obesity rather than individuals. Agree or disagree?

**Nhà nước chịu trách nhiệm**
- Obesity costs the public → treating lifestyle diseases puts a strain on the healthcare system.
- Only governments can change the environment → ban junk food near schools, build parks and cycle lanes.

**Cá nhân chịu trách nhiệm**
- Personal choices → nobody forces people to eat fast food or skip exercise.
- Parents shape children's habits → family meals matter more than laws → e.g. children who eat vegetables at home keep the habit.

> Gợi ý: cả hai, nhà nước tạo môi trường, cá nhân đưa ra lựa chọn.

### Người già được khuyên tập thể dục nhưng ít người làm

Đề (10/07/2026, Cause–Solution): Older people are advised to exercise, but many do not. Why? What can be done?

**Nguyên nhân**
- Physical limits and fear of injury → joint pain, fear of falling.
- Lack of suitable facilities → gyms are designed for the young → e.g. loud music and expensive memberships.
- No company → exercising alone feels boring.

**Giải pháp**
- Free community classes → tai chi, walking groups in parks → e.g. the morning exercise groups around Hoàn Kiếm Lake.
- Doctors prescribe exercise → regular check-ups include an activity plan.
- Family encouragement → grandchildren walking with grandparents after dinner.

## Speaking dự đoán

**Do you watch sports on TV? (Part 1 – Sports programmes)**

Mainly football, and only the big matches. Whenever Vietnam plays in the AFF Cup, my whole family **gathers round the TV** and it gets incredibly loud. I'm not really into watching other sports, but I'll **tune in** to badminton if a Vietnamese player reaches the final.

**Do you prefer watching sports live or on TV? (Part 1 – Sports programmes)**

Live, if I can **get hold of tickets**. The atmosphere in a stadium is **electric**; you're singing with thousands of strangers. On TV you get replays and a better view, of course, but it **doesn't come close to** being there in person.

**Do you eat enough fruit and vegetables? (Part 1 – Fruit & vegetables)**

I try to, but probably not as much as I should. At home my mum always serves a plate of **stir-fried morning glory** with dinner, so that's easy. The problem is lunch at work, when I often **grab a quick bánh mì**. I've started keeping bananas on my desk to **make up for it**.

**Does tiredness affect your work? (Part 1 – Tiredness)**

Definitely. If I **stay up late** the night before, I find it hard to focus in meetings and I make silly mistakes in emails. I also get more **irritable** with people. So these days I try to **switch off my phone** by eleven and get at least seven hours' sleep.

**Why are live sports events popular? (Part 3 – Sports events)**

I think it's mainly about **the shared experience**. People enjoy celebrating or suffering together with thousands of other fans. There's also the **unpredictability**: nobody knows the result in advance, unlike a film. And for many people, supporting a team is part of their **sense of identity**, so going to a match feels like belonging to a community.

**Should governments spend money on stadiums? (Part 3 – Sports events)**

Only **to a certain extent**. A national stadium is useful for big events and can **bring in revenue** from concerts too. But I'd rather see the money go to small sports grounds in every district, because that's where ordinary people actually exercise. Giant stadiums often **sit empty** for most of the year.
