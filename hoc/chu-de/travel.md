## Cụm từ

- **off the beaten track** — nơi ít khách du lịch biết — We found a small fishing village off the beaten track, about an hour from Quy Nhơn.
- **a tourist trap** — chỗ chặt chém khách du lịch — The restaurant by the lake turned out to be a tourist trap with overpriced food.
- **soak up the atmosphere** — tận hưởng không khí — I love sitting in a café in Hội An to soak up the atmosphere of the old town.
- **take in the views** — ngắm cảnh — We stopped at the top of the Mã Pí Lèng Pass to take in the views.
- **a breathtaking view** — cảnh đẹp ngoạn mục — From the cable car you get a breathtaking view of the whole bay.
- **go sightseeing** — đi tham quan — We spent the first day going sightseeing and the second day relaxing on the beach.
- **get away from it all** — trốn khỏi bộn bề cuộc sống — A weekend in the mountains is the perfect way to get away from it all.
- **a weekend getaway** — chuyến đi chơi ngắn cuối tuần — Ninh Bình is an ideal weekend getaway for people living in Hanoi.
- **travel on a shoestring** — du lịch siêu tiết kiệm — As a student, I travelled on a shoestring, staying in hostels and taking night buses.
- **a package holiday** — tour trọn gói — A package holiday is convenient, but you have very little freedom to explore.
- **backpack around** — du lịch ba lô quanh — Many young Europeans backpack around Southeast Asia for several months.
- **broaden my horizons** — mở mang tầm mắt — Travelling alone abroad really broadened my horizons.
- **a once-in-a-lifetime experience** — trải nghiệm có một không hai — Seeing the Northern Lights would be a once-in-a-lifetime experience.
- **a long-haul flight** — chuyến bay đường dài — After a twelve-hour long-haul flight, I needed a whole day to recover.
- **suffer from jet lag** — bị lệch múi giờ — I always suffer from jet lag when I fly to Europe.
- **experience culture shock** — bị sốc văn hoá — Many Vietnamese students experience culture shock when they first arrive in the UK.
- **respect local customs** — tôn trọng phong tục địa phương — Tourists should respect local customs, such as covering their shoulders in temples.
- **mass tourism** — du lịch đại trà — Mass tourism has turned some quiet beaches into crowded resorts.
- **leave no trace** — không để lại dấu vết, rác — Hikers are asked to leave no trace and take all their rubbish home.
- **space tourism** — du lịch vũ trụ — Space tourism is still only affordable for a handful of billionaires.

## Kho ý tưởng Task 2

### Hạn chế chuyến bay du lịch thay vì hạn chế ô tô

Đề (03/08 & 30/08/2026, Opinion): Long-distance flights cause serious pollution. Non-essential flights such as those for tourism should be discouraged, rather than limiting car use. Agree or disagree?

**Đồng ý**
- One long-haul flight produces as much CO2 as months of daily driving → cutting a holiday flight has a bigger impact than cutting short car trips.
- Tourism flights are a choice, while many people need cars for work → e.g. a return flight from Hanoi to London emits more than many people produce from their motorbikes in a year.
- Alternatives exist → domestic trips by train or coach.

**Không đồng ý**
- Cars are used by billions every day → total emissions from road transport are far higher than from aviation.
- Tourism supports jobs → e.g. Phú Quốc and Đà Nẵng depend heavily on international visitors.
- Fairness → flight taxes would make travel a privilege of the rich again.

> Gợi ý: "rather than" → lập luận nên làm cả hai, không chọn một.

### Du lịch có lợi hay hại cho địa phương

Đề (đề quen thuộc, Adv–Disadv): International tourism has become a huge industry. Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages for local communities?

**Lợi ích**
- Jobs and income → hotels, restaurants, guides and handicraft sellers → e.g. homestays in Sa Pa let ethnic minority families earn far more than from farming.
- Investment in infrastructure → airports, roads and clean water benefit residents too.
- Cultural exchange → traditional crafts and festivals are kept alive because visitors value them.

**Bất lợi**
- Mass tourism damages the environment → e.g. coral reefs around Nha Trang have been harmed by boats and divers.
- Rising prices → locals are priced out of their own neighbourhoods.
- Loss of authenticity → villages become stage sets and a tourist trap.

### Du lịch có trách nhiệm: ai phải lo

Đề (đề quen thuộc, Cause–Solution): Tourism is damaging many natural and historic sites. What are the causes? What can be done?

**Nguyên nhân**
- Too many visitors at the same time → e.g. Hạ Long Bay receives millions of tourists a year.
- Careless behaviour → litter, touching ancient walls, ignoring rules.
- Short-term profit → local authorities allow unlimited development of hotels.

**Giải pháp**
- Visitor limits and timed tickets → e.g. Machu Picchu sells a fixed number of entry tickets each day.
- Higher entrance fees used for conservation.
- Educate tourists to respect local customs and leave no trace, through signs and tour guides.

### Tiền cho du lịch vũ trụ và khám phá vũ trụ

Đề (23/07/2026; 03/2026, Opinion): The money spent on space exploration is not justified; it should be spent on problems on Earth. Agree or disagree? (biến thể: Space tourism is a waste of resources.)

**Đồng ý**
- Urgent problems on Earth → poverty, hunger and climate change need funding now.
- Space tourism benefits only the super-rich → a ten-minute flight costs hundreds of thousands of dollars and burns huge amounts of fuel.

**Không đồng ý**
- Space research solves problems on Earth → satellites for weather forecasting, GPS and disaster warnings.
- Technology spin-offs → e.g. water purification and medical imaging were developed for space missions.
- Private companies, not taxpayers, pay for most space tourism, and costs fall over time.

### Tìm hiểu nước khác qua internet hay đi thực tế

Đề (đề quen thuộc, Opinion): Thanks to the internet, people can learn about other countries without travelling, so travelling abroad is unnecessary. Agree or disagree?

**Đồng ý (một phần)**
- Virtual tours, documentaries and vlogs are free and avoid the cost and pollution of flights.
- Online information is often more detailed than what a short trip can show.

**Không đồng ý**
- Real experience cannot be replaced → tasting street food, talking to locals, feeling the climate.
- Personal growth → travel helps people broaden their horizons, become more independent and experience culture shock first-hand.
- Online content is selective → social media shows only the most beautiful spots, not daily life.

## Speaking dự đoán

**Would you like to travel into space? (Part 1 – Outer space & stars)**

In theory, yes, it would be **a once-in-a-lifetime experience** to see the Earth from above. But to be honest, I'd be **terrified** of something going wrong, and the price is **astronomical**, no pun intended. So for now I'm happy watching it in sci-fi films from the comfort of my sofa.

**Do you prefer travelling in your own country or abroad? (Part 3 – Place)**

At the moment I'd say my own country, because there are so many places I still haven't been to, and it's **much easier on the wallet**. You also don't have to deal with visas or **language barriers**. That said, going abroad now and then is valuable, because it **takes you out of your comfort zone** in a way domestic trips rarely do.

**How should people prepare before travelling to a new place? (Part 3 – Place)**

I think the basics are **reading up on** the local customs and checking the weather, so you pack the right clothes. It's also worth learning a few phrases like "hello" and "thank you", because locals **really appreciate the effort**. And people should always have travel insurance, just in case **something goes wrong**.

**Why do people like spending time near water? (Part 3 – Place)**

There's something very **calming** about the sound of waves or a river. Being near water usually means cooler air and more open space, which is a nice break from **the hustle and bustle** of the city. In Vietnam, a lot of people **head to the coast** every summer for exactly that reason.

**Is tourism always good for local people? (Part 3 – Place)**

Not always, I'm afraid. It obviously **brings in money** and jobs, but when a place becomes too popular, prices go up and locals can't afford to live there any more. You also see **mass tourism** damaging beaches and old buildings. I think it's only positive if it's **properly managed**, for example by limiting visitor numbers.

**Describe a city you have been to and would like to visit again. (Part 2 – gợi ý dàn ý)**

- When/what: Đà Lạt, a short trip a few years ago with friends, **a weekend getaway** from the heat.
- What you did: wandered round the night market, cycled round Xuân Hương Lake, visited flower farms and pine forests.
- What it was like: **cool and misty**, slower pace of life, French colonial villas, great coffee.
- Why go again: missed some places **off the beaten track**; want to go in the off-season to **avoid the crowds** and simply **soak up the atmosphere**.
